Energy in Elastic Systems

How hard is it to stretch a spring, and where does the energy go?

Unit 4, Lab 1

The question. Pull a spring and let it go, and it snaps back. Something was stored while you pulled, and something came back out.

Your job: find out how the force needed to stretch a spring depends on how far it is stretched, and then find a way to say how much energy is stored.

Before you collect anything

1.Your group has been given a particular spring. Pull it gently. What is the relationship you expect between how far you stretch it and how hard you have to pull? Sketch it.

Draw your own axes and label them.

2.Where is the energy going while you stretch it? Draw an energy pie chart for the spring before you pull and while you are holding it stretched.

The system is the spring. The area of the whole pie stands for the total energy stored in the system, so a bigger pie means more energy. Drag the sliders to build each pie.

Apparatus

  • Your assigned spring. Do not swap with another group, because the whole class needs different springs.
  • Spring scale, or force sensor and interface
  • Meter stick or ruler with a millimeter scale
  • A stand or clamp to hold one end of the spring

Two rules that protect the data and the equipment

Do not over-stretch the spring. Once a spring is pulled past its limit it never goes back, and every group after you gets a broken spring. If the coils start separating unevenly, stop.

Do not record a data point for an unstretched spring. The 0,0 point is not data. It is an assumption, and forcing your line through it hides one of the most interesting things this lab can show you.

Procedure

  1. Attach one end of the spring so it cannot move. Measure the length of the spring with nothing pulling it. This is your reference length.
  2. Pull the free end with the spring scale. Read the force and measure the new length.
  3. The stretch is the new length minus the reference length. It is a change, not a total.
  4. Repeat for at least six different stretches, spread across the range your spring can take safely.
